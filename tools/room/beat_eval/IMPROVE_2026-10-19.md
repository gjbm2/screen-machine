# Beat stack in the room: what improves it (held-out result and recommendation)

19 Oct 2026, screen-machine-26. Held-out test: `baseline-subset.json` (51 tracks; 46 have a steady beat), with the baseline's per-track seeds, 30 s of room sound before the music, a 260 s cap and the slow lane off. **C** = the room model with the calibrated gate (-48.64). **A** = clean audio. All tuning was done on the disjoint DEV subset. CPU = the rhythm lane's ms of CPU per second of music (one thread, nice 19, with the 4K render running). Our lane = 106 ms/s.

## 1. Verdict

**No candidate meets the C bars.** The best is **beatnet room**: BeatNet with its CRNN fine-tuned on music played through the room model, followed by madmom's online DBN as a forward filter and our line. It passes the precision bar. It misses coverage (0.43 against 0.70) and time to lock (10.3 s against 8 s).

**How far off beatnet room is:**
- Coverage needs to rise 1.6x (0.43 to 0.70).
- Lock takes 2.3 s too long: 10.3 s median over the tracks that lock, 11.6 s if the 17 % that never lock count as never.
- In C its F beats our clock on 43 of 46 tracks, and its coverage on 38 (2 worse).
- In A it is better on every steady-music column. It is worse on expressive pieces (3 of 5 false locks against 1), so it does not fully meet "no worse on clean".
- The ranking on DEV was the same, so this is not a held-out fluke.

**The real recording** (Sweet Dreams at party level through the PC22 speakerphone) is the only check against real audio.

| | music only (rec-music.wav) | whole recording (rec.wav: 164 s of room sound, 18 s of seeking, then the music) |
|---|---|---|
| our clock | cover 0.65-0.69, prec 0.99, lock 3.9 s | never locks (sits at 42-50 bpm) |
| beatnet room | F 0.97, cover 0.87, prec 1.00, lock 5.3 s | 0 beat-gated flashes in the room sound; then locks at **half tempo** (63 vs 125 bpm): cover 0.50, prec 1.00 |
| beatnet room, halve rule off (my diagnostic run, not a tuning) | - | 0 beat-gated in the room sound; cover 0.96, prec 1.00, 97 % within 25 ms |
| madmom_dbn best | cover 0.92, prec 1.00, lock 3.3 s | 0 beat-gated in the room sound; cover 0.96, prec 0.99 |
| micfe + our clock | cover 0.79, prec 1.00 | cover 0.96, prec 1.00 |

On this one track, every new candidate passes every bar except beatnet's half-tempo lock. The room model was fitted to this same recording and reproduces it (madmom F 0.96 in the model, 0.92 real). So the gap between this recording and held-out C comes from the music, not from a model that is too harsh. Sweet Dreams is an easy track: steady 4/4 with a loud synth bass. The test set has waltzes, jazz, metal and quiet songs.

**What limits it, in order:**
1. **What the network hears.** The activation's AUC for beats against off-beats in C is 0.70 for madmom's 2 nets and 0.80 for the room-tuned CRNN; clean audio gives about 0.9. With 8 madmom nets, only 49 % of beats (DEV, C) get any flash within 70 ms, and no gate or tracker can cover beats the activation does not show. The fine-tune is why beatnet wins: the same line on the stock CRNN covers 0.07.
2. **Tempo octave.** In C, beatnet's Acc1 is 0.52 against Acc2 0.85, so about a third of tracks run at half or double tempo. Half tempo still lands on beats but covers half of them.
3. **The test set couples two bars.** 38 of the 46 steady tracks are 30 s excerpts. A 10 s lock caps coverage at 0.67, so coverage of 0.7 needs a lock in about 8 s or less and nearly every beat after it. On the full songs (Harmonix, 6), beatnet covers 0.51.
4. **Genres still failing in C:** waltz, jazz, metal and blues.

**The mic, honestly.** Only our clock was run under E (the room model without the room's own sound). On the same 40 tracks:

| our clock | clean (A) | mic chain, no room sound (E) | mic chain + room sound (C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| cover | 0.56 | 0.13 | 0.07 |
| locked | 0.88 | 0.38 | 0.23 |

Talk and typing cost about half of what is left. But three quarters of the loss is there with the room silent. It comes from:
- the speakerphone's own noise suppression and echo cancelling (coherence 0.15; it keeps 51-65 % of sub and bass onsets but only 9 % mid and 5 % high);
- its cut below about 165 Hz and above 7.9 kHz;
- the low level (-48.6 LUFS);
- the room's tail.

The model reproduces the real onset loss only with that processing in it (fit error 0.023, against 1.166 with the linear chain and room sound alone). The tail (RT60 1.0 s) is fitted, not measured, because the processing hides it. **The mic is the main limit, not the tracker.**

**What a better mic would buy.** A flat mic with no processing, such as the USB boundary mic on order, removes the scramble and the bass cut. It still hears the room's tail and its sound, so the result will land between C and A. Clean audio is the ceiling, and on A the DBN trackers already meet every bar:
- best2 (today's 2 nets with the DBN, +0.02 core): cover 0.77, precision 0.91, lock 3.8 s, 0 of 5 expressive false locks;
- best (8 nets): cover 0.79, lock 3.6 s.

How close to A a real mic gets is not measured. A cheap offline estimate is possible today: run condition C through roomsim's `--no-dynamics`, i.e. the room and the mic's frequency response without the speakerphone's processing. That needs a pass-through flag on run_stack.py's `--degrade`. The real answer is a calibration of the new mic.

## 3. Recommendation and integration plan (show_service, owner screen-machine-3a)

**Recommendation:**
1. Make beatnet room the rhythm lane for `pc22-mic`, selectable per stream, with today's lane kept as the fallback.
2. First follow-up, small and measured: fix the halve rule (below).
3. When the boundary mic arrives: calibrate it, retrain the CRNN on its model, and re-measure beatnet room against madmom_dbn best2 under that mic's C and A (about 1 h of lock time). If the new mic measures near-clean, best2 needs no fine-tune and may be the simpler permanent choice.

Do not adopt micfe or the ourclock variants. Their held-out gains are small or noise: micfe is worse than our clock on 22 of 46 tracks, and ourclock covers 0.13 at 1.95x the CPU while staying flat on A.

**Replace:**
- **Activation** (beatproc child): add an activation `bnroom`. It takes the same AGC as Clock, soxr MQ to 22.05 kHz, BeatNet features (1411-sample window, 441 hop, 50 fps), then the CRNN with the stream's weights (`bnroom-r1.pt`).
  - Use `alt/beatnet_stack.py LiveLane`, plain numpy, so no torch at run time. It matches the torch model to float32 noise (its `check`).
  - Run it in 100 ms chunks.
  - madmom stays installed as the fallback activation.
- **Estimator** (clock.py): for a stream set to `dbn`, replace the peaks, PLP tempogram, alpha-beta PLL, seed, octave and cold-search rules with:
  - madmom's beat state space as an exact forward filter at 100 fps (two steps per frame), 55-215 bpm, lambda_t 100, obs_lambda 16, keeping the whole posterior;
  - our alpha-beta line on sub-frame activation peaks: alpha 0.2, beta critically damped, started by least squares, hit threshold 0.4 within 70 ms;
  - a reset rule: follow the filter at once while the line misses, otherwise only after 3 s of disagreement (tempo tolerance 0.08, phase 0.25 beat);
  - the octave halve check (above 140 bpm with margin 0.2; above 155 bpm with margin 0.1);
  - conf = hit rate x P(|err| < 25 ms) x clarity / 0.85.
  - Reference code: `alt/beatnet_stack.py` (HMM, refine, halve) and `alt/lineclock.py`.

**Keep unchanged:**
- The published clock contract and its fields. conf keeps the same meaning (a P-within-25 ms style score), so it is not a contract change; madmom_dbn would have changed it to 70 ms.
- The line's publishing: slew of half the error per beat at most 4 %, count jumps of at most half a beat, epoch.
- The gates and hysteresis: beat 0.70/0.60, dmx 0.45/0.35, 2-bar dwell.
- The quiet rule, dsp and its calibrated loudness gate.
- The slow lane, still wired. It never applied in any test. Its Beat This! input is deaf at this mic's level: 0 beats at -48.8 dBFS, the right tempo when normalised to -20 dBFS (`alt/bt_lane.py`). Normalise it before relying on it for octaves.
- **Bars and phrases:** no candidate was scored on them, and the candidates publish bar_conf 0. Keep Clock's bar and phrase code running on the new beat count and check it live.

**Follow-ups, each tuned on DEV and then confirmed on the held-out set:**
1. **Halve rule.** On the whole real recording it halved a wrong 174-185 bpm room-sound lock to 61-63 bpm, and the line then stayed at half of the song's 125 bpm: cover 0.50. With the rule off it covers 0.96. Add the reverse check: double the tempo when the midpoints between line beats carry peaks as strong as the beats. Also clear the octave state on a cold restart. The fix must keep the rule's DEV gain in C (precision 0.89 to 0.96).
2. **Expressive guard.** Add a no-steady-pulse term to conf, from the tempogram clarity Clock already computes or from CED's music and piano words. Today beatnet false-locks 3 of 5 pieces on clean audio and 1 of 5 in C.
3. **Combination worth one offline test, using the cached stage 1:** the room CRNN plus madmom's 2-net activation, averaged into the same filter and line. It has not been measured.

**Risks:**
- **Sim-to-real.** The CRNN learned one room model of one mic. The only real check is one track. Only half of the room-sound recording was held out of training.
- **The mic changes soon.** The fine-tune must be redone for the boundary mic with the same pipeline (`beatnet_train.py data`, then `train`; about 15 min of CPU, hours of wall time under lock contention). Until then that stream uses the fallback.
- **Full songs.** On Harmonix in C, beatnet's precision is 0.81 (0.89 pooled) with wrong-lock 0.14, below our clock (0.96) and best2 (1.00). There are only 6 tracks, but full songs are what the room plays.
- **Octave errors** (Acc1 0.52) and the half-tempo lock above.
- **Conf scale.** The 0.85 scale is calibrated to this room and mic.

**Tests at the build:**
- Crash-level checks.
- One parity check (shared plumbing): replay rec-music.wav and rec.wav through run_stack with the live code and compare the flashes with `beatnet_stack.py --preset room`. They must match.

**Measure live afterwards** with a new audio_cal run on the same mic and PA level:
- **Play 5-6 varied tracks, not one:** Sweet Dreams again (to compare), a waltz, a swing or jazz track, a soft acoustic song, a full-length rock or pop song, and a rubato piano piece. Put 2-3 minutes of normal room sound (talk, typing) before and between them.
- **Beats, from the bus log against the reference:**
  - time to the first beat gate;
  - coverage (beats with a beat-gated flash within 70 ms);
  - precision while gated;
  - share within 25 ms;
  - wrong-lock;
  - published bpm against the truth (octave errors);
  - beat-gated flashes a minute on room sound (target 0) and on the piano piece (target near 0);
  - conf calibration (the share of gated flashes within 25 ms, binned by conf).
- **CPU** of show_service, the ML worker and the child together. Expect 0.35-0.40 of a core.
- **Offline against live:** replay the recording offline and check that the offline flashes equal the live ones.
- **Boundary mic:** repeat all of this when it arrives.

## 4. Per-mic settings from the calibration

Key these by stream in `audio_cal.json streams.<stream>`, as the gate offset is keyed today:
1. **Loudness gate reference** (`music_lufs`, -48.64 for pc22-mic). Already live.
2. **Rhythm lane choice:** the activation (`bnroom` and its weights file with its sha, or madmom with N nets) and the estimator (`dbn` or the legacy one). A stream without its own fine-tune gets the legacy lane.
3. **conf scale** (0.85 for pc22-mic, set on DEV C so that gated precision stays at 0.9 or above). If madmom_dbn's conf is ever used instead, its logistic weights are per mic and per net count (refit with `dbn_sweep.py calib`).
4. **Training inputs for the fine-tune:** the calibration's `model.json` plus its room-sound recording. A calibration run must keep recording a few minutes of ordinary room sound.
5. **Mic lag** (10.5-10.6 ms measured): only if the clock or latency.json compensates the input lag per stream. Do not invent it for an uncalibrated mic.
6. **If CED's music word is used later** (ourclock's b, or the expressive guard): its thresholds depend on level (music_p reads 0.1-0.6 at -48.6 LUFS), so they are per mic, unless CED's input is level-normalised first.

These are generic and need no per-mic value: the DBN settings, the bpm range, the hysteresis and the halve thresholds. micfe's settings (RT60, band edges) are not needed, because micfe is not recommended.
